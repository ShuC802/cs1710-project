Definitions, data coverage and caveats
  • Data: LLM Price-Performance Tracker (Kaggle), scrape of 2026-03-31, 453 model endpoints. Nothing is imputed; every chart says how many models it had to leave out.
  • Good enough: score ≥ X% of the best score any model has in that task (best: general 57.2, coding 57.3, math 99.0). The three indexes use different scales, so a percentage gives one shared definition.
  • Monthly bill: usage tokens × the model's input and output list prices at a 3:1 input:output mix (the same mix the dataset's blended price uses).
  • Release year only: the dataset has no release dates and 161 models have no year, so time views group models by release year and skip those models. The 138 models without a price (mostly open-weight, lower scoring) cannot appear in any price view.
  • Free models are open-weight models with a $0 list price. They are shown in a separate lane and never used for "× cheaper".

Definitions, data coverage and caveats
  • Data: LLM Price-Performance Tracker (Kaggle), scrape of 2026-03-31, 453 model endpoints. Nothing is imputed; each view says how many models it leaves out.
  • Good enough: score ≥ X% of the best score any model has in that task (best: general 57.2, coding 57.3, math 99.0).
  • Budget: a model passes if its monthly bill at the chosen usage is within budget. The dotted line in ② shows the same cap as a price per 1M tokens.
  • Speed: output tokens/second, known for most but not all priced models; with a speed floor set, models with unknown speed are dropped and counted.
  • Free models are open-weight with a $0 list price (you pay for hosting yourself). They always pass the budget control.

Data. LLM Price-Performance Tracker (Kaggle), 453 model endpoints, scrape of 2026-03-31. The tracker reports only a release year, so time views group models by release year. 138 models have no price and 161 have no year; each view says what it leaves out. Nothing is imputed. Cleaning steps: dashboards/data/cleaning_log.txt.

Prices are list prices from the 2026-03-31 snapshot and may have changed since. Reasoning models often emit more tokens, so real bills can run higher.
